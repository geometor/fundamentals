F.8 Chord Intersection

If two chords AB and CD of a circle (O, R) intersect at a point X at distance r = |OX| from the center, then the product of their segments is constant and equals the power of X with respect to the circle: |AX|*|BX| = |CX|*|DX| = R^2 - |OX|^2.

F.9 Circle Equations

Pairwise subtraction of Cartesian circle equations (x - u_i)^2 + (y - v_i)^2 = r_i^2 eliminates the quadratic terms x^2 + y^2 to yield linear radical-axis relations, determining both the side Z of a square from three corner distances and the radius of a Sangaku circle tangent to an inscribed square and equilateral triangle.

F.10 Circles and Geometric Mean

In a semicircle on diameter AB = m + n, the perpendicular altitude h = CH from a point C on the circumference to AB is the geometric mean sqrt(m*n) of the segments AH = m and HB = n, while the radius OC = (m + n)/2 is their arithmetic mean.

F.12 Coaxial Circles

A hyperbolic pencil of coaxial circles with limiting points L_1 = (-r, 0) and L_2 = (+r, 0) coincides with the Apollonian equipotential circles d_1 / d_2 = k around two point charges Q_1, Q_2, sharing the y-axis as their common radical axis.

F.13 Compare Triangles in Circles

For a family of triangles ABC inscribed in a fixed circle of radius R sharing a fixed chord AB = c (with central angle 2*phi where sin(phi) = c/(2R)), the area K decomposes into the signed sum of three central isosceles triangles: K = (1/2) c h_c + (1/2) a h_a - (1/2) b h_b.

F.15 Cyclic Quadrilateral

A cyclic quadrilateral WXYZ whose interior angles form the arithmetic progression theta, 2*theta, 3*theta, 4*theta has theta = 36 degrees (W = 36, X = 72, Y = 144, Z = 108) and golden-ratio diagonals |XZ| = 2 sin(36) = sqrt(3 - phi) and |WY| = 2 sin(72) = phi*sqrt(3 - phi) in the unit circle; by contrast, no non-trivial cyclic quadrilateral can have interior angles in geometric progression theta, k*theta, k^2*theta, k^3*theta.
