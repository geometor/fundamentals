F.11 Circumcenter¶

Perpendicular Bisectors, Cartesian Determinant Formulas, Circumcircle Subtriangles, and the Tangential Triangle

The perpendicular bisectors of the three sides of triangle ABC concur at the circumcenter O = X(3), equidistant from A, B, C by the circumradius R = abc/(4K), with homogeneous barycentric coordinates a^2 S_A : b^2 S_B : c^2 S_C.

A : Point B : Point C : Point ===== M_A = midpoint(B, C) M_B = midpoint(C, A) M_C = midpoint(A, B) perp_a = perpendicular(M_A, line(B, C)) perp_b = perpendicular(M_B, line(C, A)) O = intersect(perp_a, perp_b) circumcircle = circle(O, A)

Mathematical Synthesis¶

Dated May 25 and May 31, 2011 (SeeligNotebooks), Seelig’s study of the circumcenter O \(= X(3)\) derives the exact Cartesian coordinates \((x_0, y_0)\) from vertex coordinates (correcting a sign error in Joseph O’Rourke’s published formula), transcribes Christopher Morley’s poem ‘A circle is a happy thing to be’, and decomposes \(\triangle ABC\) into the three isosceles circumcircle subtriangles \(\triangle AOB, \triangle BOC, \triangle COA\) of altitudes \(h_C = \sqrt{R^2 - (c/2)^2}\).

In Folios 5–6, Seelig constructs the tangential triangle bounded by the tangent lines to the circumcircle at A, B, C, contrasting the acute case (where the tangential triangle encloses \(\triangle ABC\)) with the obtuse case. On the canonical \((5, 6, 7)\) triangle, \(R = \frac{35\sqrt{6}}{24} \approx 3.57217\) and \(O = (7/2, 5\sqrt{6}/18) \approx (3.5, 0.6804)\).

Key Algebraic Identities¶

\[R = \frac{a b c}{4 K}, \qquad O = \left(a^2 S_A : b^2 S_B : c^2 S_C\right) = \left(\sin 2\alpha : \sin 2\beta : \sin 2\gamma\right)\]
\[D = 2\left[x_A(y_C - y_B) + x_B(y_A - y_C) + x_C(y_B - y_A)\right], \qquad x_0 = \frac{\sum (x_A^2 + y_A^2)(y_C - y_B)}{D}\]
