F.7 Ceva and Menelaus¶

Concurrent Cevians, Collinear Transversals, the MNIJ Lattice Framework, and Cevian Programs

Three cevians AD, BE, CF of triangle ABC concur at a point P if and only if (AF/FB)(BD/DC)(CE/EA) = +1 (Ceva’s Theorem), whereas points D, E, F’ on the sidelines are collinear if and only if (AF’/F’B)(BD/DC)(CE/EA) = -1 (Menelaus’s Theorem), making (A, B; F, F’) a harmonic range.

A : Point B : Point C : Point P : Point ===== D = intersect(line(A, P), line(B, C)) E = intersect(line(B, P), line(C, A)) F = intersect(line(C, P), line(A, B)) F’ = intersect(line(D, E), line(A, B))

Mathematical Synthesis¶

Spanning 26 folios (SeeligNotebooks), Topic 07 is one of the richest investigations in the Seelig archive. Combining Ceva's theorem (Giovanni Ceva, \(+1\)) and Menelaus's theorem (Menelaus, \(-1\)), Seelig demonstrates that the cevian trace F and the trilinear polar intersection F divide side A B in a harmonic conjugate range \(AF/FB = AF'/BF'\). In Folio 1 (FWS 2011/11/07), he applies Ceva's Theorem to equal tangent segments \(x = s-a, y = s-b, z = s-c\) to prove the concurrency of the Gergonne point \(G_e = X(7)\).

Across Folios 2–22, Seelig develops the MNIJ coordinate system (\(A=(0,0), B=(M,0), C=(I,J)\), with \(y\)-intercept \(N = JM/(M-I)\)), deriving closed-form rational expressions for the Cartesian trace coordinates D, E, F, the cevian lengths \(d, e, f\) via Stewart's theorem, the trilinear area of the cevian triangle \(\triangle DEF\), and a complete HP-35s calculator program (FWS 8/25/2014) verified on \((a,b,c) = (6,9,13)\) and on a Heronian \((78, 90, 84)\) triangle (April 14, 2015).

Key Algebraic Identities¶

\[\frac{AF}{FB} \cdot \frac{BD}{DC} \cdot \frac{CE}{EA} = +1 \quad (\text{Ceva}), \qquad \frac{AF'}{F'B} \cdot \frac{BD}{DC} \cdot \frac{CE}{EA} = -1 \quad (\text{Menelaus})\]
\[D_x = \frac{M J P}{J P + (M - I) Q}, \qquad E_x = \frac{M I Q}{I Q + (M - P) J}, \qquad F_x = \frac{I Q - J P}{Q - J}\]
\[\frac{PD}{AD} + \frac{PE}{BE} + \frac{PF}{CF} = 1, \qquad \frac{AP}{AD} + \frac{BP}{BE} + \frac{CP}{CF} = 2\]
